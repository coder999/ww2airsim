# Plan 7c and after: the rest of the AI — design

**Status: design for Mark's review, 2026-09-25. No code, no implementation plan
yet.** This covers the five things Mark put in scope on 2026-09-25: a named
maneuver library, formation flying with wingmen, teams and many-vs-many, landing
AI, and his ruling on the veteran pursuer ("tone the veteran down"). It builds
on 7a (controller seam), 7b (Pursue/Extend/Break decision layer) and 7d
(perception staleness and control noise). Their designs and handoffs are the
record of what exists. This document restates only what it has to re-measure.

It is too large for one plan. Completed plans here ran 6 to 15 tasks each, and
this is closer to 40, so §2 splits it into four sub-plans that each ship on
their own. **The identifiers 7c, 7e, 7f and 7g are proposals.** Master spec §15
numbering is Mark's call (Open for Mark, item 1).

Every number below was measured in this worktree on 2026-09-25 at `6b51f87`,
Node 22.22.1 on nexus, using headless Node probes. Nothing ran on the reference
GPU: the photoreal session owns that budget today. Where a number comes from a
probe, the text says how the probe worked, so the implementation plan can
rebuild it as a committed test.

## 1. What exists, measured

### 1.1 The red Tier 2 spec is reproducible at Tier 1, and the cause is narrower than the 7d handoff said

The 7d handoff says the veteran pursuer "kills a passive player at tick 517
(8.6 s, 386 m)". It blames 7d, but it could not reproduce the kill at Tier 1:
the headless `scenario.test.ts` world records only one hit, at tick 525.

The two tiers disagree because of the **player's loadout**. A page load flies
the title screen's `DEFAULT_LOADOUT`, which is `'both'` (bombs and rockets,
`src/render/titleScreen.ts`, unchanged since `89dee56` on 2026-09-22). Every
Tier 1 world is built `'clean'`. I drove the production frame path
headlessly: `initialFrameStateFor` → `nextFrameState(f, 1/60, new Set())`,
default assists, no keys, the same code path `main.ts` runs. The results:

| Player loadout | Outcome against the shipped veteran (`noiseCursor` 0) |
| --- | --- |
| `clean` | survives, 1 hit, closes to 21.5 m |
| `bombs` | survives, 10 hits |
| `rockets` | survives, 7 hits |
| `both` (the browser default) | **destroyed at tick 517, closest range 386.5 m**, exactly the Tier 2 numbers |

These results hold at 60 Hz and 144 Hz frame rates, with `step` or
`stepChecked`, and with or without terrain. **So a Tier 1 replica of the red
spec exists. It is deterministic and it matches the reference GPU to the
tick.** 7c uses it as the measuring instrument (§3.1).

### 1.2 Lethality against a passive target is an accident of aim, not a skill setting

One trajectory says little about how lethal a skill is, so I ran an ensemble:
the four loadouts × 32 starting values of `noiseCursor`, 128 runs, each ending
at the first moment the range drops below `MIN_ENGAGEMENT_RANGE_M` or at the
player's death. **The shipped veteran kills the passive player first in 4 of
128 runs.** Two knobs, measured over 32 runs each (4 loadouts × 8 cursors):

| Veteran variant | Player killed before 120 m | Mean hits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| shipped (`controlNoise` 0.02) | 1 / 32 | 1.34 |
| `controlNoise` 0.04 | 11 / 32 | 5.25 |
| `controlNoise` 0.06 | 17 / 32 | 8.00 |
| `controlNoise` 0.08 | 22 / 32 | 10.03 |
| `controlNoise` 0.01 | 0 / 32 | 0.03 |
| `controlNoise` 0 | 0 / 32 | 0.00 |
| `gunneryAccuracy` 0.3 / 0.4 / 0.8 / 1.0 | 3 / 2 / 2 / 2 of 32 | 1.3–1.8 |
| `reactionS` 0.5 / 0.7 | 3 / 10 of 32 | 2.8 / 5.3 |
| **green, shipped** | **16 / 32** | 9.31 |

**A shakier hand is deadlier, and green is more lethal than veteran.** The
cause is the aim point. Of the veteran's rounds that pass within 15 m of the
player, the mean vertical miss is **1.66 ± 0.58 m below** the target, in the
player's body frame. At `controlNoise` 0 it is 2.21 ± 0.59 m below, and the
hit count is zero. Lateral spread is ±7.5 m, because the wing guns converge at
300 m and diverge beyond it.

`muzzleLeadDirection` (`src/sim/ai/pursuit.ts`) leads for target motion but
not for gravity. A perfect aim therefore streams just under the target, and
only jitter walks rounds up into it. I prototyped gravity compensation (a
temporary edit, since reverted): raising the aim point by the computed drop
made the veteran kill the passive player in 27 of 32 runs, and at twice the
drop in 32 of 32. With compensation the veteran becomes more lethal than green
(correct ordering), and far more lethal overall.

That is Open for Mark, item 2. §3.1 carries out his ruling as given and states
what it costs.

### 1.3 The AI overstresses its own airframe and follows the player into the sea

I replayed 7d's Tier 2 scripted evasion through the same headless frame path:
roll left and pull for 4 s, reverse for 3 s, then hands off. The pursuer flies
Pursue for 100% of ticks and has no G awareness:

- It first exceeds `limits.gLimit` (7.5 G) at tick 1256 (green) or 1959
  (veteran). It peaks near 10 G and is **destroyed by its own Plan 6c
  structural overload** between ticks 2138 and 2692 (36–45 s), without taking
  a single hit.
- The player's scripted dive passes below sea level at tick 1695, and the
  pursuer follows at tick 1770. Tier 1 has no terrain, so both fly on. In the
  browser the sea would end the pursuer's flight. No AI code looks at the
  ground.

7d's acceptance bar is real, not an artifact of either defect. The player gets
behind the pursuer at 18–20 s in all 32 runs (both skills, all loadouts),
while the pursuer still has structure 1.0. Both defects surface later in the
same fight.

### 1.4 Other facts the design relies on

- **The flight controller caps bank at 70°** (`MAX_BANK_RAD`,
  `controller.ts`), and its pitch channel pushes negative G whenever the
  desired vector lies below the nose. It cannot fly a split-S or an Immelmann.
  The only way to get there is to roll past 90° and pull.
- **The approach autopilot exists, but it is a test instrument in `tools/`**
  (`tools/autopilot/approach.ts`). Its own header says the mission-AI version
  "is Plan 7's". It is a pure, phase-less function of state that flies a 3°
  path at Vref = 1.3 × `stallSpeedFlapMps`, flares at 12 m and brakes on
  roll-out. It only yaws and never banks, so it needs to start already lined
  up. Both landing tests start it lined up 4 km out
  (`tests/sim/landing.test.ts`, `tests/sim/carrierLanding.test.ts`).
- **Kills are credited without regard to side.** `creditAircraftDamage`
  (`combat.ts`) credits any kill to the shooter, and Plan 9's `missionScore`
  scores it. A player who shoots down a friendly wingman would earn 500
  points.
- **Tick cost is low.** I built a synthetic world of 8 veteran pairs dueling
  plus the player (17 aircraft) and ran 3,600 ticks of `advance`: p50
  0.05 ms/tick, p95 0.92 ms/tick. The projectile pool (`MAX_PROJECTILES`
  4096) holds about 17 aircraft firing continuously (6 guns × 800 rpm × 3 s
  life = 240 rounds in flight each).
- **Nothing in `sim/` knows about clouds.** They are renderer content
  (`scenario.ts`, `CloudLayerObject`), so any AI "sees" through them.

**7c comes first, for three reasons.**

1. The veteran retune is the one item that is red today, and it is small.
2. Its instrument, the ensemble replica of §1.2, becomes the regression gate
   for every later maneuver change.
3. It follows §15's first coupling: whatever changes *how the AI flies* comes
   before anything tuned against how it flies. Station-keeping, target
   selection and approach capture all depend on the controller and safety
   layer that 7c changes.

**7e before 7f**, because the wingman's "cover the leader" is a target
selection rule, and target selection is 7e. **7g last**, because a pair
recovering to one deck needs a landing interval, which needs formation.

7g has no hard dependency beyond 7c and could run before 7f if Mark would
rather see AI landings first. 7e, 7f and 7g must not run in parallel with each
other: all three edit the per-pilot tick in `src/sim/ai/`.

**One structural change makes the later plans cheap.** 7c moves the per-pilot
block out of `advance()` (`loop.ts`, the "Every AI reads this SAME
start-of-tick array" section) into one pure function, `pilotTick` in
`src/sim/ai/pilotTick.ts`. It takes the entity, the start-of-tick snapshot and
a small context (`nowS`, terrain, decks, wind, the combat records), and
returns the entity with its controls and pilot state updated. After 7c,
`loop.ts` is edited once more at most (7e, to pass sides into
`stepCombat`), and 7f and 7g never touch it.

## 3. Plan 7c — veteran retune, safety envelope, maneuver library

### 3.1 The veteran ruling: carried out as given

**Change:** `VETERAN_SKILL.controlNoise` goes from 0.02 to **0.01**. Nothing
else. The comment block on it is rewritten with the §1.2 measurements, dated.
Green is untouched, which is what keeps 7d's bar in place.

**Why this lever and not another.** It is the only knob in the §1.2 sweep that
reaches zero passive kills. It is data-only. It preserves every ordering test
in `tests/sim/ai/pilot.test.ts` and `noise.test.ts`: green's noise must be at
least 2× veteran's, and 0.15 vs 0.01 satisfies that. The widening of
`gunneryAccuracy` and the extra reaction time that the ruling's wording
suggests both measured *worse* than no change at all (§1.2 table).

**What it costs, stated plainly.** Against a target flying straight, the
retuned veteran averages 0.04 hits over the whole 500 m → 120 m closure. It
remains lethal only when geometry puts its stream on the target: the
deflection shot of a second pass, or a player jinking into it. The
`scenario.test.ts` "actually hits, not just fires blind" test still passes,
but with its first hit at **tick 9912** against a 12,000 budget (17%
headroom). Before the retune the first hit was at 525; with zero noise it is
7661.

7c Task 1 therefore moves that test's target skill to `green` through the
fixture, with the reason given in the test. Its claim ("the gate and the
steering agree, so rounds connect") does not depend on skill. At green it hits
at tick 370 in the same world, so later maneuver changes cannot push it to the
edge of its budget. The underlying inversion — rounds pass low, so smoother
means safer — stays in place. Item 2 of Open for Mark proposes how to remove
it.

### 3.2 Safety envelope (hard overrides, not score terms)

The safety envelope follows 7b's precedent for `MIN_ENGAGEMENT_RANGE_M`:
safety logic is an override that no weight change can outscore.

- **Load-factor limiter** on every AI `Controls` output, including the
  existing Pursue, Extend and Break. It estimates the current load factor from
  state (`|bodyRates.z| · airspeed / g`, plus the gravity term) and cuts back
  the pitch command whenever the estimate would exceed
  `G_BUDGET × spec.limits.gLimit`. `G_BUDGET` = 0.9, a tuning value measured
  in the plan. It reads the spec, so it is airframe-agnostic by construction.
  It runs *before* control noise, so a green pilot's jitter can still nick the
  limit, which is human.
- **Ground floor.** Height above the ground (`groundUnder(terrain, decks, x,
  z)`, which returns sea level where terrain is `null`) below `FLOOR_M` =
  300 m, or projected impact within `FLOOR_TIME_S` = 8 s at the current
  vertical speed, forces a `recover` maneuver: wings level, climb at the
  limiter's G until both conditions clear. Only 7g's final-approach phase is
  exempt, because landing is the point there.
- Both are checked on **every tick**, not only at rescore. Reaction delay
  models perception of the enemy, not a pilot flying into the sea.

### 3.3 The airframe envelope (why the Zero needs no special cases)

`src/sim/ai/envelope.ts` derives everything from fields every
`AircraftSpec` already carries. There is no new content field for Lane B to
fill in, so master spec §9's hand-authored `aiHint` is replaced by a derived
one:

| Derived quantity | From | Hellcat value (for scale) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| wing loading, N/m² | (empty + current fuel) · g / `geometry.wingAreaM2` | 1,780 at `testMassKg` |
| power loading, W/N | `engine.maxPowerW · propEfficiency` / weight | — |
| corner speed, m/s | `reference.stallSpeedMps · sqrt(limits.gLimit)` | 43.81 · √7.5 = 120 |
| dive limit, roll/pitch rate | `limits.diveSpeedMps`, `rates.*` | 216, 80°/s, 30°/s |

A pure `relativeEnvelope(self, target)` returns `turnAdvantage` (the target's
wing loading divided by ours: above 1 means we out-turn it),
`climbAdvantage` (ratio of power loadings) and `diveAdvantage`. It also
classifies the pairing as `turnfight`, `boom-and-zoom` or `neutral`, with
thresholds of 1.1 / 0.9 (tuning values). A Hellcat stand-in against a Hellcat
is `neutral`. When Lane B's Zero lands, a Hellcat pilot against it reads as
`boom-and-zoom` and the Zero as `turnfight`, with no code change. The Tier 1
proof uses a synthetic variant spec (the Hellcat with 1.6× wing area), because
the Zero does not exist yet.

### 3.4 A lift-vector controller alongside the velocity controller

`controlsForDesiredVelocity` stays as it is and keeps its tests: pursuit,
formation and navigation all use it. `src/sim/ai/liftVector.ts` adds
`controlsForLiftVector(state, spec, liftDirection, loadFactorG, throttle)`,
which:

1. rolls, at any bank angle including inverted, until the body-up axis lies
   along the component of `liftDirection` perpendicular to the velocity;
2. commands the pitch rate that produces `loadFactorG`, clamped by the §3.2
   limiter.

This is how a pilot actually flies vertical maneuvers. It is also the only
correct way to fly them here: the velocity controller pushes negative G for
anything below the nose (§1.4). Its unit tests mirror 7a's controller tests:
correct command signs for lift up, down, left, right and inverted; damping
opposes body rates; outputs are finite and clamped; and a dead-astern lift
direction commands a real roll (7a's `atan2` lesson).

### 3.5 The maneuver library

7b's three-way scorer is **unchanged**. It still picks an *intent*:
Pursue, Extend or Break. At the same rescore, a new selector,
`src/sim/ai/maneuvers.ts`, picks a named maneuver within that intent, from
geometry, the §3.3 relative envelope and the pilot's repertoire. With every
fact zero it still returns lead pursuit, which keeps 7b's regression floor.

| Intent | Maneuver | Chosen when (tuning values in the plan) | Flies | Ends | Tier 1 signature (proves it happened) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pursue | **lead pursuit** | default | existing `pursuitControls` | — | existing tests |
| Pursue | **lag pursuit** | overshoot risk: range < 600 m, closure > 40 m/s, target turning | aim behind the target along its track | closure < 15 m/s | closure falls while range stays > `MIN_ENGAGEMENT_RANGE_M` |
| Pursue | **high yo-yo** | overshoot risk and energy margin ≥ 0 | lift vector above the target's turn plane, then roll back down | angle-off to the target's tail back under 30° | altitude gain ≥ 100 m, closure falls, then gun-cone re-entry |
| Pursue | **low yo-yo** | target turning, range > 400 m, closure < 0 (falling behind), height above floor + 500 m | nose inside the target's turn, below its plane | closure > 0 | altitude loss, closure turns positive |
| Pursue | **attack run** | `boom-and-zoom` pairing, ≥ 300 m above the target | dive onto the target, fire through the pass, zoom climb | back above the target, or energy advantage restored | fires in the descent; afterwards regains ≥ 60% of the altitude lost |
| Break | **defensive break** | existing Break conditions | existing `breakDesiredVelocity` | — | existing tests |
| Break | **scissors** | threat within 300 m, low angle-off, both below corner speed, `turnAdvantage` ≥ 1 | a series of reversals into the threat as it crosses the 3/9 line | threat ends up ahead of the 3/9 line, or 20 s | ≥ 2 roll-direction reversals in 12 s; the threat's along-track position passes ours |
| Break | **split-S** | threat astern in gun range, ≥ 1,500 m above ground, airspeed < 0.6 × dive limit | half-roll inverted, pull through (lift vector down) | heading reversed ± 30° | heading change ≥ 150°, altitude loss 400–1,500 m, peak G ≤ `gLimit`, exits above floor |
| Extend | **extend** | existing | existing dive-away | beyond `SAFE_SEPARATION_M` | existing tests |
| Extend | **Immelmann** | replaces Extend's shallow-climb rejoin when airspeed ≥ corner speed | half loop up, roll upright, pointing back at the threat | heading reversed ± 30° | heading change ≥ 150°, altitude *gain*, exit airspeed ≥ 1.1 × stall |

**Phased maneuvers latch.** Scissors, split-S, Immelmann, the yo-yos and the
attack run carry `phase`, `enteredAtS` and a latched reference (such as entry
heading) in `PilotDecisionState`. Once entered, a maneuver holds until its
end condition or a 20 s cap. Rescoring still runs and still refreshes
perception, as 7d requires. The only things that can interrupt are a Break
forced by `threatAstern` or a §3.2 override. Without the latch a split-S
would be re-decided halfway through, every 0.3 s.

**Repertoire is skill data** (master spec §7: "green versus veteran is
data"). `PilotSkill` gains `repertoire: readonly ManeuverName[]`:

- `GREEN_SKILL`: lead pursuit, lag pursuit, defensive break, extend. This is
  7b's behavior plus lag pursuit. Green never flies vertical maneuvers, which
  keeps 7d's beatability bar unchanged in structure, not just by luck.
- `VETERAN_SKILL`: all of them.

**The envelope gates the choice.** Scissors requires `turnAdvantage` ≥ 1: a
boom-and-zoom airframe does not get into a flat scissors with a better
turner. The attack run requires `boom-and-zoom` or `neutral` with a height
advantage. 7b's `disengageThreshold`, which nothing reads today, stays
unread. It remains a hook, not a defect.

**Deliberately not built: barrel-roll defense.** Its job, forcing an
overshoot, is already done by the high yo-yo (offense) and the scissors
(defense). It is also the one maneuver in master spec §7's list whose
acceptance signature is ambiguous: a barrel roll that fails looks like a
wobble. Bomber formation station-keeping comes with 7f (§5). **Attack run**
is here because an AI attacking bombers needs it, once bomber content exists.

### 3.6 7c acceptance

**Tier 1:**

- §3.1's three lethality tests.
- Controller unit tests (§3.4).
- A **canned-geometry world for each new maneuver**, which asserts its
  signature from the §3.5 table in production `advance`. Each test asserts
  that the maneuver was actually selected *and* that its physical signature
  happened. 7a's lesson was that "fires" and "hits" are different claims;
  here "selected split-S" and "reversed heading while descending under the G

  veteran Hellcat never enters a scissors over a 120 s scenario, but does

- **Safety soak:** 120 s of the 7d evasion replica against both skills. No

  peak `stress.loadFactorG` ≤ `gLimit`. These three are the §1.3 defects,
  asserted absent.
- Determinism: two runs bit-identical; reversing the array order changes
  nothing (7a's test, extended); a `structuredClone` round trip mid-maneuver
  flies on identically.
- The golden trajectory and the landing snapshots stay bit-identical. None of
  them has a pilot.

**Tier 2:** `ai-maneuver`, `ai-pursuit` and `ai-pursuit-difficulty` are green,

## 4. Plan 7e — sides and many-vs-many

### 4.1 Content and state

```json
{ "id": "bandit-1", "spec": "f6f-hellcat", "side": "axis",
  "airborneAt": { "position": [0, 3500, -6000], "headingDeg": 180, "speedMps": 130 },
  "pilot": { "skill": "veteran" } }
```

- `side: 'allied' | 'axis'` is optional on a scenario aircraft. **When it is
  absent, the player is `allied` and every other aircraft is `axis`.** That
  rule reproduces every shipped scenario's scoring and targeting exactly: the
  gunnery range's parked Hellcats still score as kills, and `pursuer-1` is
  hostile. A scenario that wants a friendly AI says `"side": "allied"`.
  `AircraftEntity` gains an optional `side`, read through `sideOf(world,
  entity)`, so the 50-plus hand-built test entities stay valid.
- `pilot.target` becomes optional. **When present it keeps the 7a/7b static
  meaning**, but must now name an opposite-side aircraft, which the schema and
  `createWorldOf` both check. **When absent, the pilot chooses its target**
  (§4.2). `PilotDecisionState` gains `targetId: EntityId | null` and a mode
  (`engage` / `ingress` (§4.5) / `formation` / `rtb` / `landed` / `loiter`;
  7f and 7g fill in `formation`, `rtb` and `landed`).

### 4.2 Target selection

Target selection runs at rescore, alongside the intent choice, reading the
start-of-tick snapshot only. Candidates are aircraft that are opposite-side,
alive, not impacted, not parked, and within `DETECTION_RANGE_M` = 8,000 m (a
tuning value; the sim cannot see clouds, which Mark accepted on 2026-09-25, §9). The score is a
weighted sum of five terms:

- nearer is better;
- a large bonus for "this contact has me in its gun cone" (7b's
  `threatAstern`, read symmetrically);
- a bonus for "this contact threatens my leader" (7f adds the leader);
- a bonus for a contact whose tail is toward me;
- a penalty per friendly already engaging it (read from the snapshot's
  `targetId`s);
- a stickiness bonus for my current target, as hysteresis.

Ties break by id. Everything is read from the snapshot, so two pilots
rescoring on the same tick cannot see each other's new picks, and array order
cannot matter.

**Target validity is checked every tick.** A target that was destroyed or
impacted since the last rescore forces an immediate re-selection. This closes
the gap `ai-maneuver.spec.ts` records as "Finding 9", where a pilot kept
targeting a dead aircraft. A static `target` that dies leaves the pilot
without a target, not chasing a wreck. With no target, a pilot with a leader
flies formation (7f); with a home and 30 s without contacts it returns to base
(7g); otherwise it loiters, holding heading and altitude above the floor.

### 4.3 Friendly fire and credit

- **Hold fire:** the gun gate also refuses when any same-side aircraft is
  within `2 × cone` of the nose and no farther than the target plus 100 m.
- **Friendly fire stays physical.** A round still hits whatever it hits.
  `creditAircraftDamage` counts a same-side hit or kill in new
  `friendlyHits`/`friendlyKills` fields on `AircraftCombat` and **never in
  `killsByType`**, so Plan 9's scoring stops rewarding teamkills without any
  change to `debrief.ts`. Whether teamkills should *cost* points is Open for
  Mark, item 4.

### 4.4 Radar and diagnostics

- `radarContacts` (`src/render/radar.ts`) gains a `friendly` flag, derived
  from the entities it already receives. The scope draws friendlies in a
  second tint. `main.ts` does not change for this, because the contact list
  is built from the same `frame.world.aircraft`.
- `__ww2.aircraft()` gains `side`, `mode`, `maneuver` and `targetId`. This is
  a small edit to the one diagnostic function in `main.ts` (§8 covers the
  merge risk).

### 4.5 The ingress pilot (for the meta-game's Combat Air Patrol)

Added 2026-09-25 at Mark's request. The meta-game spec's Combat Air Patrol
mission sends two held waves of raiders toward the carrier. The mission fails
if any raider gets within 5 km of the carrier; that is a `deny` condition in
the sim-side mission engine (`src/sim/mission/`, owned by the meta-game spec,
not by the 7-series). This pilot flies those raiders.

**Content, kept small:**

```json
"pilot": {
  "skill": "green",
  "ingress": {
    "route": [
      { "x": -12000, "z": -30000, "altitudeM": 4000, "speedMps": 130 },
      { "x": -15000, "z": -36000, "altitudeM": 3000, "speedMps": 140 }
    ],
    "destination": { "ship": "cv-1" }
  }
}
```

- `route`: one or more waypoints in world meters. Each carries the altitude
  and speed of the leg flown *to* it.
- `destination`: optional, `{ "ship": id }` or `{ "airfield": id }`. After
  the last waypoint the pilot flies at the destination's live position, at
  the last leg's altitude and speed. For a ship that position moves, so it is
  read every tick. With no destination, the pilot holds the last waypoint.
- `ingress` excludes `target` and `leader` in the schema: a raider's target
  is chosen, never fixed. Anything else about the mission, such as the 5 km
  ring, which ship matters, and when the wave appears, belongs to the
  mission engine's content, not to the pilot.

**Behavior.** This adds a mode, `ingress`, to the §4.1 list.

- **Flying the route.** The desired velocity points at the current waypoint.
  Its vertical component closes the altitude error, clamped to ±10 m/s. Its
  magnitude is the leg's speed. It is flown through the existing velocity
  controller, under the §3.2 safety envelope. The waypoint counts as reached
  within 1,000 m horizontally, a tuning value.
- **Progress** is `legIndex` in `PilotDecisionState`, which is plain data and
  survives serialization.
- **Engaging.** At each rescore the pilot runs §4.2's target selection over
  a smaller candidate set. It engages only:
  - a contact that has it in its gun cone (`threatAstern`), or has hit it
    within the last 10 s (read from `damage.attacker` and `lastHit`); or
  - an opposite-side contact within `INGRESS_ENGAGE_RANGE_M` = 3,000 m, a
    tuning value.

  It never turns back to chase a contact beyond that range. So a raider
  fights whoever intercepts it and otherwise keeps coming, which is what
  makes the deny ring a real threat.
- **Resuming.** When its target is gone, or leaves 1.5 × the engage range,
  it resumes the route at its current `legIndex`.
- **Arrival.** After it arrives (within 1,000 m of the destination) it
  orbits there. It does not attack the ship: there is no torpedo or bombing
  AI. The mission engine's deny ring has already fired by then.

**Spawning at tick > 0.** The mission engine brings held groups into the
world mid-flight through triggers. Three things make a pilot correct on an
entity whose first tick is not tick 0:

1. **The first tick rescores.** `pilotAssignmentFrom` already seeds
   `nextRescoreS: 0`, and the rescore test is `nowS >= nextRescoreS`. So a
   pilot created at any tick rescores, selects a target and captures its
   perception snapshot on its first tick, before steering. The placeholder
   `observedTarget*` of `ZERO` is never flown against. 7e keeps that seed and
   adds a Tier 1 test that spawns a pilot at tick 3,000 and asserts that its
   first commanded tick used a real snapshot.
2. **The noise cursor is seeded from the entity id, not a constant.** Today
   every pilot starts with `noiseCursor: 0`, so two pilots spawned together
   jitter in lockstep. 7e seeds it with a fixed string hash of the id. That
   is deterministic, independent of spawn tick and array order, and the same
   whether the entity existed at world creation or was spawned by a trigger.
   This changes `pursuer-1`'s trajectory in `pursuit-range`. 7e therefore
   re-runs §3.1's lethality tests and 7d's evasion replica. They already span
   several cursor values, so the check is that the retune still holds at a new
   cursor, not that one trajectory is preserved.
3. **No pilot state refers to the world clock at creation.** `legIndex`
   starts at 0, `targetId` at `null`, and the mode at `ingress`. Phase
   latches (§3.5) record `enteredAtS` only when they are entered, so nothing
   has to be offset by the spawn tick.

How a held entity enters `World` is the meta-game spec's mechanism, not this
one's. Today `createWorldOf` rejects any entity whose `state.tick` is not 0,
so that engine has to insert entities with `state.tick` equal to the world
tick. The 7-series asks only that the entity carries a pilot built by
`pilotAssignmentFrom` and that `pilotTick` sees it in the start-of-tick
snapshot like any other.

**Acceptance (added to §4.7).**

- Tier 1:
  - A raider with no opposition flies a three-leg route. Each waypoint is
    reached in order, each leg ends within 100 m of its altitude and 10 m/s
    of its speed, and the raider ends orbiting the moving carrier.
  - An interceptor that attacks the raider mid-route makes the raider switch
    to `engage` within one `reactionS`. When the interceptor dies or leaves,
    the raider resumes at the same `legIndex`.
  - A hostile 6 km off the route never pulls the raider off it.
  - The spawn-at-tick-3000 test from item 1 above.
  - Two raiders spawned on the same tick jitter differently, which proves the
    id-seeded cursor.
- Tier 2: none of its own. The meta-game spec's Combat Air Patrol acceptance
  exercises it.

## 5. Plan 7f — formation and wingmen

- **Content:** `pilot.leader: <id>` names a same-side aircraft. A leader may
  have several wingmen. A wingman cannot also lead, so the structure is pairs
  and flights, with no chains. `pilot.slot` (1, 2, 3, …) picks a station from
  a fixed table in the leader's *heading* frame, not its body frame, so the
  wing does not swing up and down with the leader's bank. Loose cruise
  stations: slot 1 is 80 m aft and 100 m right; slot 2 is 80 m aft and 100 m
  left; and so on (tuning values). **The player can be a leader.** That is
  the friendly wingman.
- **Station-keeping:** desired velocity = the leader's velocity plus a
  clamped proportional term on position error, flown through the existing
  velocity controller. The same mechanism serves bomber formations once
  bomber content exists. There are no bombers in the roster files yet.
- **Split to attack, cover the leader.** When the leader's mode is `engage`
  (or, for the player, when the player has fired in the last 5 s or a hostile
  is within 1,500 m), the wingman leaves its station for a trail-cover
  position 500 m behind and 200 m above the leader. §4.2's leader-threat bonus
  makes a hostile threatening the leader the wingman's first choice. If none
  exists, it engages any hostile that engages it. When the leader extends or
  breaks, the leader's attacker becomes the wingman's target: the "sandwich".
  With no hostiles in detection range, it rejoins its station.
- **Leader lost:** the wingman becomes leaderless and carries on alone.
- **Content:** `furball-range` gains `leader` links, and the allied AI
  becomes the player's wingman.
- **Not built: commands to the wingman** ("attack my target", "rejoin"). They
  need key bindings, the input layer and a legend entry. Open for Mark, item
  5.

**Acceptance.**

- Tier 1:
  - station error RMS bounded with the leader straight and level, and through
    a 30°-bank turn;
  - rejoin from 2 km within a bounded time;
  - a canned case where a hostile settles behind the leader: within one
    `reactionS` the wingman targets it and range closes; the hostile then
    takes hits;
  - the player-as-leader case: the player is flown by scripted `withControls`
    and the wingman holds station;
  - §3.6's safety and determinism invariants.
- Tier 2: in the chase view, the player's wingman is within its station
  tolerance (read from `__ww2`), with a screenshot captured and read.

## 6. Plan 7g — landing AI

- **Content:** `pilot.home: { airfield: <id> } | { ship: <id> }`, validated.
  The airfield must be in the scenario and have a runway; the ship must be in
  the scenario and have a flight deck.
- **Return-to-base decision.** Checked at rescore; it pre-empts `engage`
  unless the pilot has a threat astern.
  - Triggers: fuel fraction ≤ 0.25; ammunition exhausted; `structure` < 0.5;
    or 30 s without any hostile in detection range.
  - Wingmen go home in formation with their leader.
- **Phases.** They live in `PilotDecisionState` and are chosen from state,
  following the approach autopilot's phase-less precedent where possible:
  1. `transit` to an initial point 8 km out, 600 m above the ground, at
     cruise speed;
  2. `join`: a base turn onto the extended centerline or deck axis, arriving
     at the final approach fix, 4,000 m out and 244.6 m above touchdown
     elevation (the 3.5° slope both landing tests already start from);
  3. `configure`: gear, flaps and, for a carrier, the hook, before the fix,
     at Vref + 15 m/s (Vref = 1.3 × `stallSpeedFlapMps`, 49.1 m/s for the
     Hellcat);
  4. `final`: `approachControls`, promoted from `tools/autopilot/` to
     `src/sim/ai/approach.ts`. `tools/` re-exports it so both landing tests
     stay put, and their inline snapshots must remain bit-identical;
  5. `rollout`;
  6. `landed`, which is terminal: brake 1, throttle 0 and, on a deck, hook
     down. The aircraft stays where it stopped. Plan 8 already keeps a
     braked airplane at rest relative to a moving deck.
- **Capture window at the fix:** within 60 m laterally, 10° of heading, 40 m
  of height and Vref ± 8 m/s. Outside the window the pilot **goes around**:
  climb straight ahead to 300 m above the ground, then re-join.
  - **Carrier:** the gate and aim point are recomputed every tick from the
    live deck pose. The aim is the near edge of the trap zone, which
    `carrierLanding.test.ts` measured as the right aim for this autopilot's
    float. `surfaceVelocity`, `windVelocity` and `hookDown` come from the
    existing `ApproachTarget`. **The AI obeys the LSO**: `paddlesCue`
    returning `wave-off` triggers a go-around, as Plan 8's §12 intended
    ("Plan 7's AI pilots fly `paddlesCue` directly").
- **Interval:** aircraft recovering to the same home land in id order, 60 s
  apart. A later arrival holds in an orbit at the initial point.
- **Terrain:** an airfield recovery needs `world.terrain` for touchdown
  elevation and holds at the initial point while it is `null`. Recovery to a
  deck does not need terrain at all, so its Tier 1 test runs without the
  gitignored tiles.
- **Content:** a `recovery-range` development scenario. Two allied AI with
  low fuel start 15 km out, one with home `tacloban` and one with home
  `cv-1`.

**Acceptance.**

- Tier 1:
  - Runway: touchdown inside the runway rectangle, sink under
    `MAX_SUPPORTED_SINK_MPS`, at rest on the runway, `impact` null. This test
    skips by name if the terrain tiles are absent, as the landing tests do.
  - Carrier: an arrest inside the trap zone and rest relative to the deck,
    with the paddles cue sampled every tick and at least one `roger`.
  - A deliberately bad join that must go around once and then land.
  - A wave-off injected by geometry.
  - The approach autopilot's inline snapshots stay bit-identical.
- Tier 2: `recovery-range` shows the carrier-bound AI reaching mode `landed`
  on the deck in `__ww2.aircraft()`, with zero validation errors and the
  budget held.

Landing has no effect on scoring. The recovery multiplier is the player's
alone (GAMEPLAY.md).

## 7. Cross-cutting constraints

- **Purity and determinism.** Everything new lives in `src/sim/ai/` and
  imports only from `sim/`. `.dependency-cruiser.cjs` is untouched and
  `tests/architecture/boundary.test.ts` still bites. The only randomness is
  the existing per-pilot `noiseCursor`. New choices break ties by id, never by
  array position.
- **Serialization.** Every new field is plain data, so a `World` round-trips
  through `structuredClone` (tested in 7c, 7e and 7g).
- **Airframe-agnostic.** No AI code names an airframe. Performance comes from
  the spec (§3.3), G from `limits.gLimit`, approach speed from
  `reference.stallSpeedFlapMps`, and gun ballistics from `spec.combat`.
- **No manual verification.** Every acceptance item above is an assertion.
  Tier 2 screenshots are read by the executing agent, not by Mark.

## Decisions (Mark, 2026-09-25)

**Spec approved by Mark 2026-09-25 with all five recommendations below
accepted as written** (sub-plan order 7c -> 7e -> 7f -> 7g; AI gunnery
honesty as a follow-on after the Zero lands; green restricted to the basic
set; no friendly-kill penalty; autonomous wingman).

**Added to the gunnery-honesty follow-on (shoot-down spike, 2026-09-25):**
`muzzleLeadDirection` in `src/sim/ai/pursuit.ts` leads by
`target.velocity * tof`, but rounds inherit the shooter's velocity
(`combat.ts:501,506`), so the lead must use `(target - self)` velocity.
Probe: an abeam wingman at the same velocity 300 m away is killed by a
nose-on burst while the gate is closed, and scores 0 hits at the AI's 7.7
deg lead point while the gate is open. Fixing it and the drop term makes
the AI deadlier, so the section 3.1 lethality runs are repeated in that slice.

**Execution:** the combat track runs gunnery fix -> Z2 (A6M Zero flight
model and guns) -> 7c -> 7e -> 7f -> 7g. The player-side gunnery fix changes
`pursuit-range` to a head-on green start and moves the old geometry to a
frozen test fixture; section 3.1's instruments must read that fixture.

### The items as proposed (kept as the record)

1. **Plan numbering and order.** I propose 7c (retune, safety envelope,
   maneuver library), then 7e (sides and furball), then 7f (formation and
   wingmen), then 7g (landing AI), recorded as rows under Plan 7 in §15.
   *Recommendation: this order.* 7g could move ahead of 7f if you want to
   see AI landings sooner; nothing but the landing interval between two
   aircraft depends on 7f.
2. **The AI's aim ignores gravity drop, so lethality is an accident.**
   Carrying out your veteran ruling makes the veteran nearly harmless against
   a target flying straight (0.04 mean hits across the whole closure), while
   green stays the deadlier pilot against the same target (16 of 32
   kills). Compensating for the drop fixes that ordering, but then *any*
   competent pursuer kills a passive player long before point-blank (27–32 of
   32 in the prototype). That contradicts the premise of
   `ai-maneuver.spec.ts`. Lane B's Zero, whose slower 20 mm rounds drop
   further, would make the AI even more harmless. *Recommendation:* ship the
   retune in 7c as ruled. Then add a small "gunnery honesty" slice once the
   Zero lands: drop-compensated aim plus an explicit, per-skill aim error
   (higher is worse, the `controlNoise` convention), tuned so veteran is
   deadlier than green. In the same slice, re-express the point-blank
   break-off gate with the pursuer's guns made safe (a development override),
   so it tests the break-off rather than lethality.
3. **Green's repertoire.** Should green pilots fly only 7b's maneuvers plus
   lag pursuit (no split-S, Immelmann, yo-yos or scissors), or the whole
   library, sloppily? *Recommendation: the restricted set.* It keeps 7d's
   green bar true by construction, and a pilot who never goes vertical reads
   as green in flight.
4. **Teamkills.** Kills of friendly aircraft never score (7e does this
   regardless). The open question is whether they should also *cost* points
   in the debrief. *Recommendation: no penalty for now.* The AI's hold-fire
   gate keeps AI teamkills near zero, and a penalty is a debrief/UI change
   outside the AI plans.
5. **Wingman commands.** 7f's wingman is autonomous: it follows, covers and
   rejoins on its own. Do you want command keys ("attack my target", "cover
   me", "rejoin") in the 7-series? *Recommendation: not now.* Build them only
   if flying with the autonomous wingman shows they are missing.
