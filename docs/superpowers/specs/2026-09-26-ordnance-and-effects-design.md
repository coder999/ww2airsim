# Ordnance models and photoreal effects: design

**Status: design for Mark's review, 2026-09-26.** Nothing here is built yet.
It covers two rows Mark added to master spec §15 the same day, "Effects
realism" and "Ordnance models". It proposes four plans, O1 and E1 to E3 (§8).
Plan numbering stays with §15; these IDs are proposals until they are written
into those two rows.

Mark's decisions on 2026-09-26, taken in conversation and taken as given here:

1. **Scope is the whole effects family.** One effects system replaces all six
   of today's effect implementations (§1). It does not ship next to them.
2. **The bar is modern sims.** "Photoreal" means comparable to IL-2 Great
   Battles, DCS or Microsoft Flight Simulator. Lit, soft, textured smoke and fire
   that hold up close in the follow view. It is judged side by side on the
   reference GPU.
3. **We build our own ordnance models.** They are generated by script from
   published dimensions. We do not take them from Sketchfab.
4. **Approach A: lit flipbook particles.** They are baked from our own Blender
   fluid simulations. Volumetric smoke that shares the cloud renderer's lighting
   (approach C) is a possible later upgrade. It is not part of this design.

## 1. What exists today

Verified 2026-09-26 by reading the code at `main`.

- **Every effect is an unlit quad or a stack of unlit cubes.** There are six
  implementations:
  - `src/render/scene/impactEffect.ts`: crash, a camera-facing quad that grows
    and fades.
  - The impact pool in `src/render/ordnance.ts`: bomb, rocket and structure
    fireballs, the same quad plus `createSmokeColumn`'s four grey cubes.
  - `src/render/scene/hitFlash.ts`: gun hits and kill fireballs.
  - `src/render/scene/smoke.ts`: engine smoke, six cubes.
  - Ship fire, the engine-smoke curve reused by `ship.ts`.
  - Building collapse, `createSmokeColumn` reused by `airfield.ts`.

  All are `MeshBasicMaterial` with `transparent`, drawn inside the scene pass.
- **The renderer guesses ordnance impacts.** `nextOrdnanceImpacts` sees a bomb
  or rocket vanish from `combat.projectiles` and fires an orange fireball at its
  last position. It cannot tell land from water, so a bomb in the sea makes a
  fireball. It also cannot tell a detonation from a `lifeS` expiry.
- **Gun rounds that hit land or water make nothing.** `combat.ts` computes the
  exact contact `point` in its `nearestContact` resolution, then uses it only
  for damage.
- **The crash already knows its surface.** It is `player.impact.surface`, which
  is `'water' | 'land' | 'deck'`.
- **Stores are primitives.** Bombs are `BoxGeometry` and rockets are
  `CylinderGeometry`, hung by `src/render/scene/stores.ts` and drawn in flight
  by `ordnance.ts`.
- **The stores hang in the wrong place.** `RACK_OFFSETS` and `RAIL_OFFSETS` were
  written for the retired procedural Hellcat. On the Wildcat model, which every
  aircraft now draws, they sit behind the tail. The H2 handoff found this
  (`docs/handoff/2026-09-26-h2-hangar-bench.md`, "Open").
- **The bomb is the AN-M65 1,000 lb GP and the rocket is the 5-inch HVAR.**
  Both come from `content/aircraft/f6f-hellcat.json`'s `stores.types`. The one
  bomb candidate in `ASSETS.md`, `bomb-m64-500lb.glb`, is the wrong bomb. It
  will not be used.

## 2. Ordnance models (plan O1)

### 2.1 Generator

`tools/models/generated/an-m65.ts` and `hvar.ts` each build their store from:

- a revolved profile (the body)
- fins, suspension lugs and fuzes (the bomb)
- motor tube, fins and warhead (the rocket)

Every dimension is a cited figure in the script's header, in the same standing
as the flight model's `source` strings. Each script writes a `.glb` through the
gltf-transform `Document` the model pipeline already uses
(`tools/models/document.ts`).

Materials:

- Olive-drab painted steel. The AN-M65 carries a yellow nose band.
- Base textures come from CC0 painted-metal sources, fetched and pinned by md5
  exactly as `tools/textures/sources.ts` does.
- Output is deterministic: the same script and inputs give byte-identical
  `.glb`.

### 2.2 Manifest

Today `tools/models/manifest.ts` requires three things:

- a Sketchfab `source.url` with a uid
- a CC license
- an `input` in `tools/models/cache/`

It gains a second source kind:

```json
"source": { "kind": "generated", "generator": "tools/models/generated/an-m65.ts",
            "dimensions": "<citation>", "license": "AGPL-3.0-or-later" }
```

Output goes to a new `content/ordnance/`. Existing entries default to
`kind: "sketchfab"` and must build byte-identically. Each generated model gets
an `ASSETS.md` row.

### 2.3 Runtime

- `stores.ts` hangs the real models on racks and rails.
- `ordnance.ts` draws them in flight, still as one `InstancedMesh` per kind,
  using the model's geometry.
- The rocket motor flame stays a primitive until E2 moves it to the `flame`
  sheet.
- **Rack and rail offsets move from shared constants into each airframe's
  content file.** They are measured for the Wildcat model in the Hangar.
  `hellcat.ts` and `wildcat.ts` stop sharing one table.

**Out of scope (recorded, not fixed):** every scenario flies Hellcat loadouts
on a Wildcat model. A late-war FM-2 did carry six HVARs, but its bombs were
lighter than the AN-M65. The loadout-per-airframe question belongs to
whichever plan gives the Wildcat its own flight model.

### 2.4 Hangar

`hangar/library.ts` gains an `ordnance` category next to aircraft, ship and
building. Each store gets the turntable and a count against its manifest
budget. The H2 stores bench is how placement is checked.

## 3. The effects system (plan E1)

```
sim step ──▶ World.combat.impacts (new, bounded)            crash · kills · ship fire · structures
                    │                                              │
                    └──────────────▶ fx/events.ts (pure) ◀─────────┘
                                          │ one-shot triggers + continuous emitters
                                          ▼
                            fx/catalog.ts (recipes, data) ──▶ fx/system.ts (pool, CPU, seeded)
                                                                   │ instance buffer
                                                                   ▼
                                                  fxPass (after the scene pass, before clouds)
```

### 3.1 Sim: surface the impacts it already computes

The combat step appends each detonation to `World.combat.impacts`. This is a
bounded ring, stamped with the tick. Each entry is:

```ts
{ tick, cause: 'round' | 'bomb' | 'rocket',
  outcome: 'detonated' | 'expired',
  surface: 'water' | 'land' | 'deck' | 'aircraft' | 'ship' | 'structure',
  point: Vec3 }
```

A frame can span several ticks, and the ring means the renderer misses none of
them. `outcome: 'expired'` marks a bomb or rocket that reached `lifeS`, which
is not an impact.

**Acceptance gate: purely additive.** The golden trajectory, the nightly soak
and every flight test card must stay bit-identical. Nothing in `sim/` reads the
ring. If a golden serializes `World.combat`, the ring is excluded from that
serialization rather than the golden being re-recorded.

### 3.2 `src/render/fx/events.ts` (pure)

This file maps sim state to effect triggers. It uses `hitFlash.ts`'s edge
memory and its restart rule: a tick that moves backwards is a new flight.

- **One-shot triggers:**
  - new `impacts` entries
  - the player's crash (with its surface)
  - kills (`destroyedAt` edges)
- **Continuous emitters, keyed by entity id, following state:**
  - engine smoke per airframe (engine health)
  - fire per ship (`damage.fire`, `sinkingFraction`)
  - collapse dust and smoke per structure

`nextOrdnanceImpacts` and its guesswork are deleted.

### 3.3 `src/render/fx/catalog.ts` (data)

A recipe is a list of emitters. Each emitter carries:

- its flipbook sheet
- count, or rate for continuous emitters
- lifetime, initial speed and spread
- size over life, tint
- drag and buoyancy (gravity for spray and debris)

The catalog is validated by a zod schema. Recipes:

| Recipe | Emitters |
| --- | --- |
| `bomb.land` | fireball, dirt ejecta (streaks), dust ring, smoke column |
| `bomb.water` | water column, spray crown, base surge, foam stamp (§5) |
| `rocket.land` / `rocket.water` | smaller versions of the above |
| `round.land` | dust puff |
| `round.water` | spout |
| `round.deck` / `round.structure` / `round.ship` | spark flash, small dust |
| `crash.land` / `crash.water` / `crash.deck` | fireball or water column at crash scale |
| `kill.air` | fireball, then a smoke trail attached to the falling wreck |
| `engine.smoke` | continuous, attached to the airframe, rate and darkness from engine health |
| `ship.fire` | continuous `flame` plus `smoke` plume scaled by `fire` |
| `structure.collapse` | dust burst, then a lingering smoke column |

Sizes and durations are cited figures where a source exists (§5.1). Otherwise
they are marked as estimates, as `stores.source` already does.

### 3.4 `src/render/fx/system.ts`

- A fixed-capacity particle pool. The capacity comes from the tier (§4.4).
- It simulates on the CPU with a seeded generator, so Tier 1 can test it
  without a GPU.
- It uploads one instance buffer per frame and draws in one batch.
- When full, it recycles the oldest particle, as `hitFlash.ts` recycles
  flashes.
- It sets raw world metres, and camera-relative rendering is inherited from
  `scene.position`, as every effect does today (`main.ts`'s note on why).
- **GPU compute is not built.** A few thousand particles is cheap on the CPU.
  Moving the simulation to TSL compute is justified only by a Tier 2
  measurement that shows the CPU upload or simulation in the frame time.

### 3.5 Deletion

When E1 lands, these go:

- `impactEffect.ts`
- `hitFlash.ts`'s quads (its pure `nextHitFlashes` edge logic moves into
  `fx/events.ts`)
- `smoke.ts`
- `ordnance.ts`'s impact pool
- `createSmokeColumn`

`ordnance.ts` keeps only the stores in flight.

## 4. Rendering, lighting and cost

### 4.1 `fxPass`

Effects render in a `TempNode` after the scene pass, in `cloudPass.ts`'s
pattern:

- `updateBefore` draws the particle batch into a reduced-resolution target that
  reads `sceneDepth`.
- `setup` returns a depth-aware upsample composite over the scene color.

Reading depth gives two fades:

- **Soft particles:** a sprite fades out as it approaches the opaque surface
  behind it, so no hard line where smoke meets ground, water or a hull.
- **Near fade:** sprites within about 2 m of the eye fade out. Flying through
  smoke must not become a full-screen overdraw wall.

### 4.2 Ordering with clouds

The effects composite **before** the clouds. `fxPass` also writes a
reduced-resolution "dense depth": the view-Z of the nearest texel where
accumulated alpha exceeds 0.5. The cloud pass takes the minimum of that and the
scene depth as its march limit, exactly as it already stops at opaque surfaces.

Without this, a fireball in open sky has the cloud *behind* it composited
*over* it. Today's transparent effects have that defect. Air kills against a
cloud background are the common case.

### 4.3 Lighting

- **Smoke, dust and spray: six-way lightmaps (§6).** They are weighted by
  `sunDirectionNode` and `sunColorNode` in the particle's frame. Ambient comes
  from `skyIrradianceUp` / `skyIrradianceDown`. The sprite is multiplied by the
  cloud-shadow `node(position, 'eyeRelative')` and finished with
  `aerialPerspective(dir, dist)`, the same haze the clouds use, all from
  `scene/lighting.ts` and `atmosphereShading.ts`.
- **Fire: emissive HDR from a temperature ramp.** It is unaffected by sun
  shading and feeds the existing bloom.
- **No dynamic lights.** A detonation does not light nearby terrain or
  aircraft.

### 4.4 Quality tier

`fx` becomes a fourth `QualitySettings` system next to ocean, scenery and
clouds (`src/render/quality.ts`, bound in `main.ts` like the others).

| Tier | Particle cap | fxPass resolution | Frame blending | Top mip |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| high | 4,096 | ½ | yes | yes |
| medium | 2,048 | ½ | yes | yes |
| low | 1,024 | ¼ | no | skipped |

### 4.5 Cost gates

`tests/e2e/fx-budget.spec.ts` is a new stress scene. It runs in the follow
view about 300 m from the target and includes:

- 8 bombs and 32 rockets
- gunfire on land and on water
- a burning ship
- a collapsing building
- one air kill

The gates:

- **At 1440p on high, gpu p95 with effects is at most 1.0 ms above the same
  scene with `fx` disabled.**
- The existing 1440p 6.0 ms tripwire and `budget4k.spec.ts`'s limits stay
  green.

## 5. Water (plan E3)

### 5.1 Splashes

- **`bomb.water`:**
  - a tall white water column sized to the charge
  - a spray crown at its top
  - a low base surge ring racing outward
  - a foam stamp left behind
- **`rocket.water`** is the same at a smaller scale.
- **`round.water`** is a small spout. A burst lays a stitched line of them
  across the sea, which is also miss feedback.

Spray particles fall under gravity and are removed at sea level.

Column heights, crown widths and durations are read from public-domain US
federal photographs of near-misses and strafing runs (NHHC, NARA; 17 U.S.C.
§105, the rule the missions design §6.3 already set). The catalog cites them.
The look is judged by eye (Tier 3).

### 5.2 Foam stamps

The ocean has no world-anchored foam input today. Its foam is per-cascade and
comes from the FFT tiles (`ocean/wgsl.ts`, combined in `ocean/mesh.ts`).
`ocean/mesh.ts` gains a pool of 16 **foam stamps**:

- They are uniforms `{center, radius, age}` evaluated in the shader.
- They add to the existing term as `foam = max(foam, stamps)`.
- A stamp expands and fades over about 30 s.

That adds no texture and no displacement. Users: bomb and rocket splashes, a
ditching or crash in the sea, and a sinking ship.

Out of scope: wave displacement, wakes, reflections of effects in the water.

## 6. Baked flipbooks (plan E2)

### 6.1 Pipeline

`npm run fx:bake` runs `tools/fx/bake.ts`. It drives Blender 5.0.1 headless on
nexus (`blender -b -P tools/fx/blender/<sheet>.py`):

- one sheet at a time
- never concurrently with a test suite (the 2026-09-25 OOM incidents)

Baking is slow and does not run in CI. Outputs are committed, with the Blender
version, the scene file hash and the simulation seed in a manifest,
`content/fx/sheets.json`.

| Sheet | Simulation | Consumers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `fireball` | Mantaflow gas, fire + smoke | bomb, rocket, crash, kill, structure |
| `smoke` | gas, smoke only, dark and oily | smoke columns, engine smoke, kill trail, ship plume |
| `dust` | gas, smoke only, earth-toned | land impacts, collapses, `round.land` |
| `water-column` | Mantaflow liquid with spray particles | `bomb.water`, `rocket.water`, `crash.water` |
| `spray` | liquid spray and mist particles | crowns, base surge, `round.water` |
| `flame` | small steady fire | rocket motor, `ship.fire` |

Debris and sparks are code-drawn streak sprites and need no sheet.

### 6.2 What each sheet contains

For every simulation frame, an orthographic camera renders six passes. Each
is lit by a single light on ±X, ±Y or ±Z. They are packed as:

- **Lightmap A:** RGBA = right, left, top, alpha
- **Lightmap B:** RGBA = bottom, back, front, emission (fire temperature)
- **Motion:** RG = frame-to-frame motion vectors, for blending between frames

Each image becomes a KTX2 texture array with one layer per frame, built with
the existing `ktx create --layers` path (`tools/textures/`). It is loaded like
`loadSurfaceTextures`.

### 6.3 Size gates

- About 64 frames at 256² per sheet.
- **Total `content/fx/` download at most 10 MB.** `fx:bake` measures it and
  fails above that. For scale, the terrain textures are 2.77 MB.
- The low tier skips the top mip.

### 6.4 Placeholder sheets (E1)

E1 ships with sheets generated in code: noise blobs in the same three-image
layout, written by the same KTX2 path. The system, pass, lighting, ordering
and cost gate are therefore proven before any Blender work. E2 replaces the
files and changes no code outside `tools/fx/` and the catalog's tuning.

## 7. Testing

**Tier 1 (Node, no GPU)**

- Sim: golden trajectory, soak and every test card bit-identical with
  `impacts` present.
- Bomb and rocket `lifeS` expiry recorded as `outcome: 'expired'`.
- A gun round on water records `surface: 'water'` at the contact point.
- `fx/events.ts`: every source in §3.2, the restart rule, and no trigger lost
  when one frame spans several ticks.
- The catalog validates, and every recipe names an existing sheet.
- `fx/system.ts`: deterministic under a seed, oldest-first recycling, and each
  tier's cap respected.
- Generated models: byte-identical rebuilds, and measured dimensions within
  1% of the cited figures.
- Manifest: `kind: "generated"` accepted, and existing Sketchfab entries
  unchanged.
- Bake: KTX2 headers (layer count, sRGB where required) and the 10 MB gate.

**Tier 2 (reference GPU, RX 6700 XT)**

- `fx-budget.spec.ts`: §4.5's gates.
- Captures of a bomb on land, a bomb on water, and an air kill, each asserting
  effect pixels present.
- Soft edge: no single-row luminance step where smoke meets the ground.
- Cloud ordering: a fireball in front of a cloud keeps its pixels.
- Foam: a stamp raises ocean luminance at the impact point and decays by 30 s.
- Hangar: stores inside the Wildcat's wing silhouette, not behind the tail.

**Mark's viewing: a checkpoint, not a gate**

The automated tiers above decide pass or fail. Per `CLAUDE.md` ("How Mark
works"), each plan's header records two things, agreed with Mark when the
plan is written:

- when he views: intermediate tasks, the final product only, or none
- whether the run is attended

This design proposes the end of E2 and the end of E3 as natural checkpoints.

At a checkpoint:

- The work is served on `ww2airsim.windomlane.org`, with reachability asserted
  first (`curl` expecting `200`).
- The Tier 2 captures go next to reference screenshots from IL-2 Great Battles,
  DCS or MSFS.
