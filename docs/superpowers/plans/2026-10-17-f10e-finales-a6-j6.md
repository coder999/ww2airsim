## New in this batch

- **`preventRelease {targets, count}`:** an attacker counts when it was destroyed before its combat record shows a release or a drop (A6: at least five of ten). It reads the same records as F10-0's `released` and F10D's drop counts. It is E's one engine primitive; it is small and local.
- **Everything else is composition** of F10-0's `field`, `window`, `atLeast`, `groupGone`, `hold relativeTo`, population validator and egress-free landing sets, and F10D's `drop`.

## Chapter notes

**A6 Battle for the Airfields** (P-38L, dawn). `takeoff` from Tacloban -> `preventRelease {attackers, 5 of 10}` -> `field {Buri, Bayug, San Pablo, serviceable, min: 2}` through the relief timer (a window) -> `destroy {lodgment vehicles or supply groups, count: 6}` -> `land` at Tacloban, Bayug or Buri while serviceable (a landing set with a field-state condition). Secondary: all three fields remain serviceable at debrief. State transition: the existing A6 success row (all three fields stay American; Tacloban and Dulag continue). Route groups admit the ten bombers and transports as slots open (Decision, F10-0 T0.9).

**J6 Operation Te-Go** (Ki-43-IIb or A6M2, dawn, alternate history). `hold relativeTo` the bomber pair -> `released {Ki-21 or G4M pair}` -> `drop {Ki-57, 2 follow-on drops}` (F10D) -> destroy or `deny` the P-38 response -> a trigger `then setSide` and `field` drives Buri and Bayug to Japanese control once the drops and the response suppression complete -> hold both through the reinforcement timer (a window) -> `land` at Japanese Buri or Bayug. Secondary: San Pablo Japanese too, and both stay serviceable. State transition: the existing J6 alternate row. The briefing and the epilogue **name the divergence** (successful J5/J6 coordination) and carry F3's honesty label.
