## A3: time-of-day picker on Form 2

Today the hour comes only from the scenario's `weather.timeOfDay` (or DEV `?timeOfDay=`).

1. **Daylight window (pure).** `daylightWindow(latDeg, dayOfYear)` in `sky/sun.ts`: sunrise and sunset from the sun model the sky already uses (`cos H0 = -tan φ tan δ`), rounded inward to the quarter hour. For Leyte on day 294 that is **0615 to 1745** solar time. `clampToDaylight(hour)` clamps a pick into it.
2. **Form 2 field.** Under the mission list: a "Takeoff time" `<input type="time">` (step 15 min, min/max from the window). It defaults to the scenario's historical hour, read from the same scenario fetch the briefing uses, with the line "Suggested: 0730, the historical hour". Choosing another scenario resets it to that scenario's suggestion, as a scenario change already resets the aircraft and loadout. Scenarios with no hour suggest 1200.
3. **Through to the flight.** `SortieChoice` gains an optional `timeOfDay`. `main.ts` takes `?timeOfDay=` (DEV) over the pick, and the pick over the scenario's hour, on both the reload and the same-scenario paths.

## A4: quality auto-detect

Today `adaptOceanQuality` takes one p95 of GPU timestamps over the first 180 frames, **on the title screen**, against thresholds calibrated with vsync off. A vsynced Chrome idles and downclocks the GPU between frames, so the same work reads about 3x slower and Mark's desktop got Low (incident 2026-09-20, `clouds.md` #2).

1. **Measure the flight, not the title.** The probe collects only while the title is down, no scenario swap is pending, the chart and replay are closed, and the tab is visible. It skips a warm-up of 120 frames (shader compiles and first terrain streaming), then reads 240 frames.
2. **Measure what vsync cannot fool: the frame rate.** The probe reads rAF intervals, not GPU timestamps. A downclocked GPU is one with headroom: the driver raises the clock the moment a frame risks its deadline, so the frame interval reports whether the machine holds its frame rate at the tier in force, vsync or not. Median interval: **High at 50 fps or better (≤ 20 ms), Medium at 30 fps or better (≤ 33.4 ms), else Low.** It is the existing no-timestamp fallback's rule, made the only rule, with the median rather than the p95 so one hitch cannot decide it. Intervals over 250 ms (a hidden tab, a debugger) are dropped.
3. **One bundle.** The verdict sets ocean, scenery, clouds and effects together (`defaultQualitySettings`), as the probe already does. It stays a one-time, downward-only choice that never overrides a deliberate pick.
4. **Production stops tracking GPU timestamps.** Only the probe needed them in production, and their per-frame resolve serialization would skew the probe's own frame rate. DEV keeps them for the budget tests, paused while the probe collects.
5. **A quality step in the launch flow.** Form 4 (Ordnance) gains a "Render quality" row: High / Medium / Low, stamped "Recommended" on the probe's verdict. Before any measurement it reads "Measured in your first seconds of flight". A pick there is the same explicit choice as a Settings pick (`selectSimpleTier`). The recommendation is persisted (`ww2airsim.qualityRecommended.v1`), so the stamp survives a reload; until now it was lost, and only the resulting settings were saved.
6. **Say what happened.** The console prints the verdict and the median it was based on, and the GA `quality_tier` event keeps `detected` (the verdict) against `chosen` (what is in force after it), now with `fps`.

## Tests

- **Deterministic:** `daylightWindow` and `clampToDaylight`; the probe rule, as a pure function over intervals: boundaries, dropped outliers, too few samples; the draft keeping and resetting the hour; the recommendation persisting.
- **E2E (`settingsUi.spec.ts`):** the fresh-profile case now launches a sortie and expects the Recommended stamp after the probe window, not on the title. The launch-flow spec checks that the Form 2 field shows the scenario's hour, that a picked hour reaches `__ww2.sun().timeOfDay`, and that the Form 4 quality row applies a pick.

## Decisions for Mark (defaults taken, change any)

1. **Latest takeoff is sunset (1745).** Sim time runs during a sortie, so a 1745 takeoff flies into dusk with no night lighting. The alternative is ending the window an hour before sunset.
2. **The thresholds are 50 and 30 fps.** A 120 Hz display at High needs the H0 budget, but High at 60 fps is a better game than Medium at 120.
3. **The probe still swaps tiers live, once, a few seconds into the first sortie**, as it did on the title. The alternative, recommending only and applying at the next launch, keeps a weak machine on High for its whole first sortie.
4. **Hour shown in solar time, 24-hour ("0730")**, matching the briefing.
5. **The Mark's-Chrome Tier 2 assertion** (incident item 4) is a follow-up run on Ryzen.
